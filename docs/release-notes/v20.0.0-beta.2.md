The second beta of rcssmonitor-web, a browser-based monitor for the RoboCup Soccer Simulator 2D server (rcssserver). It adds a 3D view of the game to the features of rcssmonitor.

**This is a pre-release** for trying it out and for feedback. The major version follows rcssserver: this beta is for the asio-based rcssserver that will be released as version 20, the first with WebSocket connections. The stable 20.0.0 will come when the repository moves to the rcsoccersim organization; the GitHub Pages address below changes then.

## Downloads

- **rcssmonitor-web-v20.0.0-beta.2.html**: the whole app in one file. Save it and open it from disk (`file://`) to replay logs and to connect to a server anywhere on your LAN.
- **rcssmonitor-web-v20.0.0-beta.2-dist.zip**: the multi-file build, to serve from your own web server.
- The same version runs on GitHub Pages (https://hidehisaakiyama.github.io/rcssmonitor-web/), for logs and servers on `localhost`. A page served over HTTPS cannot open `ws://` connections to other machines.

## New: the 3D view

View > 3D View (D) or the 3D button of the tool bar shows the game in 3D, from logs and live games alike. It needs WebGL; three.js is loaded the first time it is opened.

- **Cameras** (View > 3D Camera, the tool bar, E for the next one): free (drag to turn, Shift+drag to move, wheel to zoom), broadcast, close-ups of the ball and of the selected player (the distance and the elevation in the 3D tab; the wheel changes the distance), top, behind either goal, and the selected player's camera.
- **Viewpoints of the whole pitch**: I puts the free camera at the side of the pitch, Ctrl+I at the next of nine (the far side, behind either goal, high above, the four corners); also in View > 3D Camera and the tool bar.
- **The selected player's camera** looks over its shoulder and turns smoothly with its face; it can draw only what rcssserver tells that player (by the server's rules, or by its view cone), or everything. The Player Camera tab of View > Preference sets its styles (at the eyes, how it turns, its field of view).
- **As in 2D**: the players in their state colors with the cards, the view areas, lines for the body and face directions, and the overlays of the Show tab on the ground (the focus point, the catch, tackle and kick areas, pointto, the offside lines, the ball's path, the player types). The stamina and its capacity are bars above the heads (S goes round them as in rcssmonitor). From the top camera, the numbers are placed and sized as the 2D view writes them.
- **Referee commands** aimed in 3D: a right click on a player aims at it (for the cards); on the ground, at that point of the pitch.
- **Human figures** (the 3D tab: Players): the CC0 Mannequin by Quaternius, which walks and runs by the speed and kicks, tackles, catches, points and falls when fouled by the log, the same when paused, stepped or played back.
- **Telling the ends apart**: the goal nets, the boards around the pitch and the banners floating behind the goals are in the team colors; a compass and a minimap can be shown.
- **The look** (the 3D tab, each can be turned off): the shadows of the players and the goals from the sun, the grass mowed in stripes, a sky, simple stands along the touch lines with a crowd in the team colors, and a football that rolls.
- **Finding the ball**: a ring on the ground around it (0.7 m by default) and a trail like a contrail over its last cycles.

The ball is drawn at its height and with a shadow below it when the show has one, for the 2.5D simulator planned for rcssserver; today's servers and logs keep it on the ground.

## Fixed

- The keepaway area was not drawn in keepaway games (the server parameter is `keepaway`, not `keepaway_mode`).

## Not included

Binary logs (v1–v3), the UDP / TCP transports and the S-expression protocols, and Qt-specific features (window style, quit, auto quit).

## Known limitations of this beta

- The 3D view keeps 60 fps at 1920×1080 in Firefox on a desktop GPU with everything on. Without a GPU (software rendering), it is much slower; turning off the shadows helps the most.
- Tested with headless Chrome and Firefox on Linux, against the replay server and against the asio-based rcssserver on the same machine. Not tested yet: browser shortcuts that the app takes over (Ctrl+1–0, Ctrl+I, Ctrl+L, F11 and others) in desktop browsers, connections from another machine on the LAN, connections from the GitHub Pages version to `localhost`, and Safari.
